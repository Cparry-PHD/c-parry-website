Why do we want to make things our own?

I'm Christian Parry, an assistant professor of marketing at Cal Poly in San Luis Obispo. I study customization: when people want to put their own stamp on a product, when they don't, and how identity and loneliness shape what we buy.

I also study digital consumption and sports, from in-game purchases and virtual currencies to athlete endorsements. I earned my PhD in Marketing from the University of Iowa's Tippie College of Business in 2026, where I was advised by Rebecca Krause-Galoni and Alice Wang. Before that I studied marketing and economics at Utah State University. I grew up in Wooster, Ohio.

Featured paper

Be Yourself, but Only if You Can't Beyoncé: Consumer Disinterest in Customizability for Celebrity Merch

Christian Parry and Rebecca Krause-Galoni

People almost always prefer products they can customize, but not when a favorite celebrity made the product. Across five studies (N = 1,728), customizing celebrity merch feels like erasing the celebrity's essence from the product, which is much of what makes it appealing.

Competitive paper, Association for Consumer Research, 2025 · Preparing for submission to Journal of Consumer Psychology

  1. Loneliness and Problematic Gaming Parry, Gopinath, Wang, and Cole International Journal of Research in Marketing

    Using text analysis of a subreddit for people trying to quit gaming, archival data, a lab experiment, and a longitudinal study, we show that lonely people are more easily transported into online games, more likely to develop gaming disorders, and spend more on digital items for their avatars.

  1. I Like Myself, but Hate the Product: Self-Sentiment in Online Reviews Alavi, Parry, Nozari, and Wang Target: Journal of Marketing Research

    When clothes don't fit the size a shopper expected, ratings drop, even when returns are easy. Encouraging shoppers to keep a positive view of their bodies keeps ratings stable and reviews useful. Lab experiments plus a language-model analysis of Amazon Fashion reviews.

Dissertation

Driving Customization: Investigating the Impact of Designer Essence and Loneliness on Customization. Two essays on the psychology behind customization: celebrity merch dampens it, and loneliness amplifies it, as a way of coping. Co-chaired by Rebecca Krause-Galoni and Alice Wang, University of Iowa.
